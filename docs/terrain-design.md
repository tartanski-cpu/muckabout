# Terrain engine design (M1)

Short version: everything at rest is a layered heightfield owned by a worker; main keeps a read-only
mirror for queries and rendering; material only becomes particles (clods) while airborne.

## Data layout (`src/terrain/core.js`, no DOM/three dependency)

World = `nx × nz` height samples on a `cell` = 0.25 m lattice (480 × 480 for the 120 m map), grouped
into 32 × 32 chunks for dirty tracking and upload. Structure-of-arrays, index `c = j*nx + i`:

| Array | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `bed` | Float32 | immovable bedrock height |
| `nl` | Uint8 | number of layers (0–3) |
| `lm`, `lt` | Uint8 / Float32, ×3 | layer material and thickness, stored bottom → top |
| `h` | Float32 | cached surface = bed + Σ thickness |
| `dist` | Uint8 | disturbed (0 = virgin, 255 = worked) for colour |
| `rut` | Float32 | compaction depth already applied by tyres/tracks |
| `wet` | Uint8 | 1 if under water (lower angle of repose) |

Adding a fourth layer merges the bottom two (volume conserved; the thinner one takes the thicker
one's material). Materials and their properties live in `materials.js`.

## Relaxation

A FIFO work list of dirty cells (ring buffer + `queued` flag, so each cell is in it at most once).
Processing a cell compares it with its 8 neighbours in both directions; where the slope exceeds the
top material's talus (wet or dry) a fraction of the excess top-layer material moves downhill and both
cells are re-queued. No sweeps: an untouched map costs nothing, and "awake chunks" is just the set
of chunks the work list touched this tick. A per-tick cell budget bounds worst-case time; leftover
work carries to the next tick.

## Commands and results

The sim is driven by one message per fixed 60 Hz step, so it is deterministic for a given command stream.

```
main → worker  {t:'init', map, seed}
               {t:'tick', n, cmds:[...]}        cmds: cut | deposit | compact | (M5) water params
               {t:'audit'} {t:'snapshot'}
worker → main  {t:'tick', n, results:[...], chunks:[{id, h:Float32Array, info:Uint8Array}], stats}
               {t:'audit', ...}
```

- `cut`: a tool floor rectangle given as previous and current world corners. The core sweeps it in
  sub-steps of half a cell (no tunnelling), removes everything above the floor, gives up to
  `capacity` m³ to the caller (per material) and windrows the rest ahead of the lip. Returns taken
  volumes and a resistance figure (Σ volume × dig resistance).
  A blade (`heap`: its height above the edge) pushes against its face instead and is a wall for relaxation while it
  is there; see "Blades" below.
- `deposit`: clod landings, bilinear into 4 samples.
- `compact`: tyre/track footprints; soft materials rut, the lost volume is booked as `compacted`.

Chunk payloads are whole 32 × 32 blocks of `h` plus `info` (top material, disturbed, wet, layer count),
transferred, then written into the mirror and into two world-sized textures with one sub-image upload each.
`TerrainClient` hides whether the core runs in a worker or inline (`?worker=0`, tests, fast-forward).

## Containers and clods (main thread)

- `ContainerField`: a small heightfield in the container's own frame with per-edge wall heights. It
  relaxes against world-up expressed in that frame, so tilt makes material run to the low edge and
  over it; whatever leaves becomes clods at that edge. Bucket, dumper bed and hopper are the same class.
- `Clods`: SoA ballistic particles `{material, volume}`, budget 1,500. They sample the mirror and the
  containers; landing in a container adds to it, landing on ground queues a `deposit`.
- Every transfer between heightfields is a clod, except tool → own container on a cut.

## Rendering

- Ground: 5 × 5 tiles of 96 × 96 cells sharing two static grids (full and half resolution, with
  skirts). The vertex shader reads height with `texelFetch` from an R32F texture; the fragment shader
  derives flat normals from derivatives and colour from the info texture. No geometry rebuilds.
- Chips: three instanced meshes (gravel shards, mud clumps, grass tufts) filled from per-chunk lists
  generated by hashing cell + material, for chunks near the player; a chunk's list is rebuilt when it changes.
- Clods: one instanced mesh, per-instance tint by material.

## Budget plan (Quest 3, 13.9 ms)

| Item | Budget | How it is held |
|---|---|---|
| Worker tick | ≤ 6 ms | relax cell budget per tick; cut cost ∝ tool footprint |
| Main sim | ≤ 3 ms | clods O(n) with no clod–clod tests; containers ≤ 300 cells each |
| Upload | ≤ 0.5 ms | only dirty chunks; 4 KB + 4 KB per chunk |
| Draw calls / tris | ≤ 150 / 400k | 25 ground tiles max, LOD halves the far ones, 3 chip meshes, 1 clod mesh |

## Tests

`tests/terrain*.test.js` run the core directly: volume conservation (ground + containers + clods +
lost + compacted constant within 0.1 %) over scripted cut/dump sequences, repose angles per material,
swept cuts not tunnelling, determinism (same commands → identical arrays).

## Water

`src/terrain/water.js`: shallow water (virtual pipes) on a 0.5 m grid over the whole map, in tiles of 16 × 16 water cells (one terrain chunk each). Only awake tiles are stepped: water flowing into a sleeping tile wakes it within the same step, so none is lost; a tile dry for 120 steps sleeps again. Sources come from the map: the river (filled at the start, an inflow at the west edge and a free outflow at the east), `springs` (a rate at a point) and `ponds` (filled to a level at the start). The worker sends the main thread only the awake tiles (`pack`), and those put to sleep since; the main thread keeps a whole-map copy (`WaterMirror`) for depth queries, drawing and co-op. Saves hold the awake tiles' water. Today's river: 31 of 225 tiles awake, 0.6 ms a step.

Friction: a light damping tuned on the river, and Manning's (n = 0.035), which grows with speed and bites in shallow water, so a stream on a steep bed runs a few centimetres deep at a metre or two a second instead of as a sheet. River erosion was retuned with it (`erodeK` 0.009), so a mud ford still washes out in a couple of minutes.

## Maps

`src/terrain/maps.js`: a map is `cfg` (grid), placement data, and `build(core)`, which fills every column. Today's map is `dirtColumn(x, z, edge)`. The valley (`src/terrain/valley.js`) uses it unchanged inside today's square and builds the ground round it from named FEATURES: each adjusts the surface `g` and flags (bare, rock, surface material, silt, spoil, added material) only within its own area, then the column's layers are made from the flags. Watercourses are cut to a bed falling evenly along their line. Water sources (`springs`, `ponds`, `streams`) and `woods` (for the scenery) are map data.

## Rock

A material can be `solid` (rock): `cut` never takes it, only what lies on it (`softAbove`), and adds a heavy resistance for the rock the edge meets. With `cmd.rip` (a ripper tooth) `cut` instead breaks the rock it reaches into its `loosens` material (shot rock) where it lies: the column down to the tooth is taken off and put back, the rock part as shot rock and anything that lay on it on top, so volumes balance. The breakout widens with depth (`RIP_BREAKOUT`, 45°). Materials are sent to co-op clients in 4 bits (bog, material 8, used to arrive as nothing in 3).

## Blades

`cut` with `heap` (the dozer: `machines/dozer.json`, `tools[].heap` = 1.15 m, the blade's top above its edge). Why and
the sources: `docs/blade-soil.md`.

- What it cuts goes into the two cells just ahead of its face (60 / 40), at the same place across the blade. Sweeping
  those again next step is what pushes the heap; nothing is thrown.
- For the step, every cell under the blade takes soil from relaxation only up to the edge's height (`wallT`, `wallY`):
  the heap cannot run back through the blade, but a hollow it passes over fills to the edge, which is how it grades.
  The strips just beyond each end, from the back of the blade to 0.7 m ahead of it, take none at all, so the heap stays
  in front until it is full.
- Soil against the face higher than the blade's top runs along the face and off the nearer end, a share a tick
  (`SPILL_RATE`), into the cells beside the end, where relaxation lays it out as a windrow. Ground beside the end that
  stands above the blade's top a metre out is a slot's wall: nothing goes off that end. Soil more than `OVER_TOP` above
  the top topples over it and lands just behind the blade.
- The result also has `fresh` (soil cut from below the blade's own heap: `pushT`/`pushB` mark cells its heap has
  reached and the ground under it), `load` (m³ in the heap ahead, out to 1.6 × height), `face` (mean height against the
  face), `spill`, `over`, and `dig` (resistance from cutting alone). `resist` adds dragging the load and the wedge
  pushed ahead of the face (∝ face²), as a force while the edge moves.
- The machine (`Machine.post`, `pre`): `digRate` (all of it) lugs the machine and loads the engine; `cutRate` (cutting
  alone) is what slows forcing the edge deeper. On the custom model the load also stalls the tracks once dragging it
  passes their grip (`SOIL_DRAG`); on Rapier the blade's force grows with `digRate` up to its `maxForce`.
- `tools/bladeprobe.mjs` prints all of this for a shallow cut, a blade held down, and a standing pile;
  `tests/blade.test.js` holds the figures.
